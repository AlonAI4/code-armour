# Legal: ways a vibe coder gets sued, fined or blocked (UK/EU/US focus)

Researched 2026-10-04. NOT legal advice. Laws differ by country and change often: every rule must be filtered by
where the builder lives, where users are, and what the project does. Key: S site, G game, A AI app, St store.
Severity = how likely enforcement or a lawsuit is for a small indie project, NOT whether the law applies. A "Low" law still applies. Re-verify anything marked unverified.

## 1. The builder's own age (check first if the builder is under 18)
- Stripe: a parent/guardian must own the account before it can charge or pay out; Connect Express/Custom holders 18+. (St/G/A, High blocker) https://support.stripe.com/questions/age-requirement-to-create-a-stripe-account
- Apple & Google developer accounts: 18+. A minor publishes under a parent, who is then the legal developer. Never lie about age. (G/A, High blocker)
- AI API accounts: usually 18+ (unverified exact floors). Parent holds the account. (Med)
- Minors' contracts are voidable (US common law; England Minors' Contracts Act 1987): suppliers and clients will want an adult or company signing. (Med)

## 2. Privacy & data protection
- GDPR / UK GDPR: applies if you target or monitor EU/UK users, wherever you live. No small-business exemption for core duties: lawful basis, privacy notice, user rights (delete/export), security, processor DPAs (Supabase/Netlify/Stripe have them). (all, High if accounts or emails) https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/
- Breach notification: tell the regulator within 72h if a breach risks people; tell users if high risk. Most common cause: Supabase RLS off or service_role key in frontend. Have a one-page incident plan. (all, High)
- UK ICO data protection fee: £52/yr tier 1, most UK controllers must pay; unclear for under-18 hobbyists (usually parent/company is controller). (Low–Med)
- CCPA/CPRA (California): only if >$26.6M revenue, OR 100k+ CA consumers' data bought/sold/shared, OR 50%+ revenue from selling data. Small projects usually exempt. (Low)
- Texas TDPSA: SBA-defined small businesses are exempt from most of it, but even they must get consent before SELLING sensitive data (kids' data, precise location). Don't sell data. (Low–Med)
- COPPA (US under-13): applies if child-directed or you know a user is under 13. Verifiable parental consent before collecting personal info. 2025 amendments (compliance by 22 Apr 2026) add biometrics/voice and separate consent for ad sharing. ~$53k per violation. Age gate, no ad trackers on kids' pages. (G/A/S, High for games)
- UK Children's Code (Age Appropriate Design Code): services likely used by under-18s in UK: high-privacy defaults, geolocation off, no nudges, DPIA. (G, Med–High)
- GDPR Art. 8: a child's own consent counts from 13–16 by country (UK 13, DE/NL 16); below that, parent consents. (Med, unverified per country)
- Cookies (PECR/ePrivacy): the EU needs prior consent for all non-essential cookies/trackers, with "reject" as easy as "accept". UK (from 5 Feb 2026, Data (Use and Access) Act): first-party analytics, functionality and security cookies are exempt IF you explain them and offer an opt-out; GA4, ad pixels and session replay still need consent. UK PECR fines now up to £17.5M/4%. (all, Med)
- Germany: loading Google Fonts from Google's servers without consent → €100 court award (LG München 2022) and waves of demand letters. Self-host fonts. (Low–Med)

## 3. Email & marketing
- CAN-SPAM (US): honest headers, physical address, working unsubscribe, honour opt-outs within 10 business days. (Med)
- PECR/ePrivacy: marketing email needs opt-in (soft opt-in only for existing customers). Unticked box, keep consent logs. (Med)
- Fake reviews: banned in UK (DMCC Act, from 6 Apr 2025, CMA fines up to 10% turnover), FTC rule in US, EU UCPD. Never write/buy reviews or hide negatives. (Med)
- Influencer/affiliate disclosure: label as #ad (FTC Endorsement Guides, UK CAP Code). (Low–Med)

## 4. Selling: consumer law, subscriptions, payments
- 14-day withdrawal right (EU/UK): for digital content, get the buyer's express consent to instant delivery AND their acknowledgement that they lose the right. Without that consent, they can cancel and pay nothing for content already used. If you never told them about the right at all, it extends by up to 12 months (UK CCR 2013 reg 31). Checkbox + confirmation email. (St/G/A, Med)
- Auto-renewing subscriptions: FTC click-to-cancel rule vacated Jul 2025, but state auto-renewal laws (e.g. California) and ROSCA still apply. UK DMCC subscription rules expected Jan 2027 (date has slipped). Clear terms, explicit consent, easy cancel (Stripe customer portal), renewal reminders. (Med)
- Drip pricing/hidden fees banned in UK (DMCC). Show full price up front. (Low–Med)
- Chargebacks: Stripe closes accounts with high dispute rates (~0.75–1%, unverified). Clear descriptor, refund policy, receipts, Radar. (Med)
- PCI DSS: never let card data touch your server; Stripe Checkout/Elements keeps you at SAQ A. Never build your own card form. (Low if Stripe-hosted)
- Physical goods: EU General Product Safety Regulation (Dec 2024) labels, traceability, EU responsible person. (St physical, Med, unverified)

## 5. Tax
- EU VAT on digital services: non-EU sellers owe VAT from the first EU consumer sale; EU sellers past €10k/yr cross-border. UK: non-UK sellers owe UK VAT from first sale; UK sellers £90k threshold. Easiest fix: a merchant of record (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, app stores) or Stripe Tax + OSS. (St/G/A, Med–High once selling abroad)
- US sales tax nexus: mostly $100k sales per state; digital goods taxable only in some states. (Low)
- Income tax: declare earnings (minors often via parent). UK £1,000 trading allowance; US self-employment tax over $400. (Med, unverified)

## 6. Accessibility
- European Accessibility Act (from 28 Jun 2025): e-commerce and some digital services to EU consumers, ~WCAG 2.1 AA. Microenterprises providing services (<10 staff AND ≤€2M) are exempt, which covers almost every solo dev. An accessibility statement is good practice and lowers ADA risk for US stores, not usually an EAA duty. (Low–Med)
- ADA Title III (US): no size exemption; 2,000+ web lawsuits in H1 2025, ~69% against online retailers. Alt text, contrast, keyboard nav, labelled forms, accessible checkout. Don't rely on "overlay" widgets. (St, Med)

## 7. Intellectual property
- Copyright in assets (sprites, music, fonts, images, code): most common indie takedown; US statutory damages up to $150k per work. Font files are copyrighted (OFL is fine). Keep a CREDITS/licences file with source + licence of every asset. (all, High)
- AI output: purely AI-generated work isn't copyrightable in the US (Thaler v. Perlmutter 2025), and outputs copying characters/styles can still infringe. Add human input; don't prompt for named IP. (Med)
- Open-source licences: MIT/Apache keep notices; GPL = release source if you distribute; AGPL = if you MODIFY it and offer it over a network, release your modified source. `npx license-checker`, NOTICES file. (Med)
- Trademarks/name clashes: names like "Pokémon"/"Minecraft" or confusingly similar to a registered mark → cease-and-desist, store removal, domain dispute. Search USPTO, UKIPO, EUIPO TMview and app stores before naming. (Med)
- DMCA safe harbour (US): if users upload content, register a designated agent ($6, renew every 3 yrs), notice-and-takedown, repeat-infringer policy. (UGC, Med)
- Likeness/deepfakes/voice cloning: right of publicity (US states, e.g. Tennessee ELVIS Act), UK passing off. Never use a real person's face or voice without permission. (A, High)

## 8. User-generated content & safety
- UK Online Safety Act: ANY user-to-user service (chat, forums, multiplayer chat, UGC) with UK links; no small-service exemption. Illegal-content risk assessment, children's access assessment, reporting tools. Fines up to £18M/10%. If you don't need chat/UGC, don't build it. Duties: a WRITTEN illegal-content risk assessment and a children's access assessment (keep the records), plus reporting and complaints tools. Exempt: comments or reviews only on your own content (OSA Sch. 1 para 4). (G/S with UGC, High)
- EU Digital Services Act: hosting services reaching the EU need a point of contact, clear terms, notice-and-action, reasons for removals; non-EU providers need an EU legal rep. Micro/small exempt from heavier duties. (Med)
- US TAKE IT DOWN Act (from 19 May 2026): UGC platforms must remove non-consensual intimate images incl. AI deepfakes within 48h on request. (UGC/image-gen, Med–High)
- Defamation from user posts: US Section 230 shields hosts; UK has no such shield once notified. Report button + fast takedown. (Med)

## 9. AI-specific
- EU AI Act Art. 50: tell users they're talking to an AI from 2 Aug 2026. Machine-readable marking of AI images/audio/video applies from 2 Aug 2026 for NEW systems; systems already on the market before then get until 2 Dec 2026 (AI Omnibus deal, 7 May 2026; confirm it was formally adopted). Label deepfakes. Fines up to €15M/3%. (A, Med)
- California SB 243 (from 1 Jan 2026): companion chatbots must disclose AI, have self-harm crisis protocols, break reminders for minors, no sexual content for minors; users can sue for $1,000 per violation. (A companion/character apps, High)
- AI provider usage policies: banned uses (sexual content involving minors, unsafeguarded medical/legal advice, impersonation) → key terminated. (A, Med)
- Health/finance/legal claims: FTC needs substantiation; personalised investment advice may be regulated (SEC/FCA). Disclaimers; don't present AI output as professional advice. (Med)

## 10. Games
- Loot boxes: illegal gambling in Belgium (2018); Netherlands narrowed in 2022 (unverified); app stores require odds disclosure. Sell items directly, disclose odds, or geo-block. (G, Med)
- Gambling/sweepstakes/skin betting: prize + chance + paying = illegal lottery. US sweepstakes need "no purchase necessary" + official rules. (G, High if real money/cashout)
- App store rules: their in-app purchase for digital goods, age ratings, privacy labels, in-app account deletion, Texas/Utah/Louisiana app-store age-verification laws (Texas in force after the 5th Circuit stayed the injunction, 28 May 2026; it puts duties on developers too, e.g. using the store's age signal and parental consent). Breaking them = removal. (G/A mobile, Med)

## 11. Business identity & paperwork
- Impressum (Germany/Austria): commercial sites must show name, address, contact (§5 DDG). Private sites exempt. (Low–Med)
- Terms of service: without them, no disclaimer, no right to ban users, no governing law. Generate, then match to real features. (Med)
- Scraping: behind logins can breach CFAA/contracts/EU database rights; scraped personal data needs a GDPR basis. Use official APIs, respect robots.txt. (Med)
- Sanctions/export controls: no selling to sanctioned countries/persons; Stripe screens payments but free downloads/crypto aren't screened. (Low)
- Personal liability: sole trader (or minor via parents) has no shield; a company/LLC separates personal assets once revenue is meaningful. (Med)

## 13. Added after legal review (2026-10-04)
- US wiretap/pixel lawsuits (California CIPA): Meta/TikTok pixels, session replay and third-party chat widgets without consent attract mass demand letters against small US-facing sites. Don't use them, or gate them behind consent. (all US-facing, High) [K]
- Truth in privacy/security claims: FTC Act §5 (US) and UK/EU GDPR transparency. Every promise in your privacy policy or marketing ("encrypted", "we never share") must match the code. False promises are the most common US enforcement hook for small apps. (all, High)
- EU/UK representative (GDPR Art. 27 / UK GDPR Art. 27): a builder outside the EU (or UK) who targets users there needs a representative, unless processing is occasional and low-risk. (all with EU/UK users, Med) [K]
- Kids paying in games: FTC v. Epic ($245M, 2022) for dark-pattern purchases and voice chat on by default. No tricky buttons, easy refunds, chat off by default. Contracts with children are voidable, so expect refunds and chargebacks. (G, Med) [K]
- AI apps sending personal data to the AI provider: say so in the privacy policy, turn off provider training/retention where possible, and respect the provider's minimum user age (e.g. OpenAI: 13+ with parental consent). (A, Med)
- GDPR Art. 8 (child consent ages) only applies when your legal basis is consent. Accounts run under "contract" are a different analysis; kids' codes and COPPA still apply. (Low)

### US privacy lawsuits (laws that let individuals sue)
- **TCPA (texts/calls):** marketing or automated SMS to US numbers needs prior express written consent; users can revoke "by any reasonable means" (STOP, email); Do-Not-Call covers texts; $500–$1,500 per text, class actions common; ~12 states add stricter "mini-TCPA" laws (Florida FTSA, quiet hours). No small-business exemption. Prevention: no marketing SMS; for codes/reminders an unticked consent box, honour STOP in any wording, send 8am–9pm local. (S/St/A with SMS, **High**) [V] https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/newsletters/tcpa-tracker/tcpa-tracker-march-april-2026.md
- **Biometrics (Illinois BIPA; Texas CUBI, Washington [K]):** face geometry and voiceprints need written notice, a written release BEFORE collection and a public retention policy; no size threshold; $1,000/$5,000 per person (2024 amendment, applied retroactively Apr 2026); face-filter/try-on tools have been sued. Prevention: process faces/voices on-device only and never store templates; otherwise consent screen + retention policy, or geo-block Illinois. (A/G/Mob with camera or voice, **High**) [V] https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/04/08/seventh-circuit-limits-potential-damages-under-bipa-holds-2024-amendment-applies-retroactively/
- **VPPA (video + pixels):** a site that plays video, has "subscribers" (accounts, sometimes even a free newsletter) and sends viewing data to Meta or other pixels faces $2,500-per-person class actions; Supreme Court took *Salazar v. Paramount* (decision 2026–27). Prevention: no ad pixels on video pages, or separate opt-in. (S/St with video, **Med–High** in US) [V] https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/01/supreme-court-to-clarify-meaning-of-consumer-under-vppa/
- **Consumer health data (Washington My Health My Data; Nevada, Connecticut similar [K]):** no revenue threshold, private right of action; "health" is broad (fitness, periods, mental health, diet, AI symptom/therapy chats); consent to collect, separate signed authorisation to sell. First class action targeted an ad SDK. Prevention: no ad/analytics SDKs in wellness features, separate consent and notice. (A/Mob wellness, **High**) [V] https://www.orrick.com/Insights/2025/02/First-Lawsuit-Filed-Under-Washingtons-My-Health-My-Data-Act
- **Precise location and minors' data:** Maryland MODPA (Oct 2025, 35k consumers) bans selling sensitive data incl. location within 1,750 ft and ANY data of under-18s; Oregon (Jan 2026) bans selling precise location and under-16s' data. Prevention: coarse location only; never share location with ad networks. (Mob/G, **Med**) [V] https://constangy.com/constangy-cyber-advisor/maryland-online-data-privacy-act-is-broader-stricter-and-more-easily-triggered-than-many-state-privacy-laws
- **Data brokers (California Delete Act):** selling data about people you have no direct relationship with (scraped profiles, people search, enrichment) means registering ($6,000/yr) and, since 1 Aug 2026, processing DROP deletion requests every 45 days; $200 per consumer per day. Prevention: don't build scrape-and-sell products. (A/S scrapers, **Med** if it applies) [V] https://cppa.ca.gov/data_brokers

### US kids, teens and age verification
- **State kids' design and social-media laws (Oct 2026):** California AADC partly enforceable since 12 Mar 2026 (age estimation, high-privacy defaults); SB 976 addictive feeds largely in force (age verification 2027); Nebraska AADC in force (AG penalties from 1 Jul 2026); Vermont AADC 1 Jan 2027; Florida HB 3 (no accounts under 14, consent 14–15) enforceable; Virginia SB 854 (1 h/day under-16) blocked, on appeal; Utah app-store duties delayed to 6 May 2027. Most have thresholds that exempt tiny projects [K], but copy their defaults: no infinite feed or autoplay for minors, no night notifications, private by default. (G/S social, **Low–Med**) [V] https://ktslaw.com/insights/alert/2026/3/ninth-circuit-allows-key-provisions-of-the-california-age-appropriate-design-code-act-to-take-effect
- **Adult-content age verification:** upheld by the Supreme Court (*Free Speech Coalition v. Paxton*, Jun 2025); about half of US states require real age checks where ~a third of a site is sexual material; UK OSA similar. A teen dev should not build or host adult content, including unfiltered NSFW AI image or chat generation. (A/S, **High** if it applies) [V] https://www.scotusblog.com/cases/free-speech-coalition-inc-v-paxton/
- **AI companions beyond California:** New York's law (since 5 Nov 2025) requires a "not a human" notice at the start and at least every 3 hours, plus self-harm detection and crisis referral; NY A10379 (no companionship for minors) awaiting the Governor at last report. (A companion/character bots, **High**) [V] https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/alerts/privacy_and_data_security_alert/all/new-york-laws-raise-the-bar-in-addressing-ai-safety-the-raise-act-and-ai-companion-models
- **Kids' advertising (CARU):** ads and in-app purchase prompts aimed at under-13s: no "deceptive door openers", no pressure to buy, ads labelled as ads; non-cooperators are referred to the FTC. (G kids, **Low–Med**) [V] https://bbbprograms.org/media/insights/blog/developers-and-caru-advertising-guidelines
- **Colorado AI Act:** delayed, then stayed by a federal court (Apr 2026); replacement proposes 1 Jan 2027; only "high-risk" AI making consequential decisions (jobs, loans, housing, education). Don't build AI that decides those. (A, **Low**) [V] https://natlawreview.com/article/colorado-takes-major-step-towards-rewriting-its-ai-law-its-effective-date

### Selling, subscriptions and money
- **California auto-renewal (AB 2863, from 1 Jul 2025)** (replaces "CA ARL unverified" above): covers free-trial-to-paid; express consent kept 3 years, ANNUAL reminder, notice before price changes, online cancel as easy as signup; ~30 other states have their own [K]. Prevention: Stripe customer portal, timestamped consent checkbox, yearly reminder emails. (St/A subscriptions, **Med**) [V] https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/california-expands-automatic-renewal-law-new-requirements-now-in-effect
- **Sweepstakes-casino bans:** California AB 831 (Jan 2026), NY, NJ, CT, MT ban "dual-currency" sweepstakes games (CA up to $25k per violation). Never build "coins you can redeem for prizes". Real giveaways: official rules + free entry; NY/FL registration and bond over $5,000 in prizes [K]. (G/S, **High** if it applies) [V] https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102lrox/california-bans-online-sweepstakes-casinos
- **Crypto, tokens, NFTs:** EU MiCA transition ended 1 Jul 2026 (exchange/custody/transfer for EU users needs authorisation); fractional or large-series NFTs can be in scope; in the US a token sold with a promise of profit can be an unregistered security [K]; Apple requires IAP for NFT sales; Chrome Web Store bans prediction-market extensions (Aug 2026). Teen dev: no tokens, no wallets holding user funds. (G/A/Ext, **High**) [V] https://cms.law/en/bel/legal-updates/nfts-under-micar-are-they-regulated-or-not
- **Virtual currency and gift cards [K]:** in-game currency that can't be cashed out is generally not money transmission; cash-out or real-money trading needs licences; US CARD Act (gift cards ≥5-year expiry); unspent balances can be unclaimed property. Keep currency closed-loop. (G/St, **Low–Med**)
- **Stripe restricted businesses [K]:** adult, gambling/sweepstakes, crypto, get-rich-quick, fake engagement, some NSFW AI: account closure and funds held up to 120 days. Read the list before launch. (St/A, **Med**)
- **Fake social influence [K]:** the FTC reviews rule also bans buying/selling fake followers or views (~$53k per violation). (S/G marketing, **Low–Med**)

### EU product rules (2026)
- **Cyber Resilience Act reporting (since 11 Sep 2026):** a "manufacturer" of a product with digital elements sold in the EU must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents via ENISA's Single Reporting Platform: 24 h early warning, 72 h notification, final report within 14 days of the fix, including products already on the market. In scope: commercially sold downloadable software (Steam games, paid apps, extensions) [K for exact edges]; out of scope: pure websites/SaaS and free open source outside commercial activity. Monetising (paid, ads, paid support) makes you a manufacturer. Full duties (CE marking, SBOM, updates) ~Dec 2027 [K]. Prevention: SECURITY.md with a contact, dependency updates, a 24 h incident plan. (G/Mob/Ext/M sold in EU, **Low–Med**) [V] https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/14/enisa-cra-single-reporting-platform
- **New EU Product Liability Directive (products placed on the market after 9 Dec 2026):** software and AI are "products"; strict liability for defects including missing security updates and data destruction; free open source outside commercial activity exempt, but "free in exchange for personal data" counts as commercial; ToS can't exclude it. Prevention: ship security fixes, offer export/backups. (commercial software in EU, **Low**) [V] https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/eu-introduces-comprehensive-digitalera-product-liability-directive
- **EU Data Act:** only connected (IoT) products and companion apps; micro/small enterprises exempt from sharing duties. (IoT apps, **Low**) [V] https://gleisslutz.com/en/data-act-qa

### Platform rules that get apps removed (removal is the real penalty)
- **EU "trader" status on app stores (DSA):** Apple removed EU apps without a declared status (Feb 2025). Earning any money (paid, IAP, ads) makes you a trader, and your address, phone and email show on the EU product page. A teen uses a parent's business details or a business address, never a minor's home address. (Mob, **High blocker**) [V] https://techcrunch.com/?p=2967719
- **Apple App Store:** privacy manifests for third-party SDKs; "required reason" APIs declared; in-app account deletion; honest age rating; set `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption`=false if you only use HTTPS/OS encryption (custom crypto needs US export classification) [V/K]. (Mob, **High** rejection) https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/security/complying-with-encryption-export-regulations.md
- **Google Play:** target API 36 from 31 Aug 2026 (extension to 1 Nov 2026); new personal accounts need a 12-tester, 14-day closed test; Data safety form must match every SDK; Android developer verification (sideloaded apps too) in BR/ID/SG/TH from 30 Sep 2026, global in 2027 (student/hobbyist tier exists). (Mob, **High** blocker) [V] https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk
- **Chrome Web Store (from 1 Aug 2026):** collect only data strictly necessary for the single disclosed purpose; disclose prominently and notify of changes; no remotely hosted code (fetch JSON fine, executing it not); bans on AI-guardrail-bypass and prediction-market extensions; MV2 purged; Firefox similar [K]. (Ext, **High** removal) [V] https://developer.chrome.com/blog/cws-policy-updates-2026
- **Steam and itch.io:** Steam requires AI-content disclosure (pre-made and live-generated; code assistants exempt) and guardrails for live generation; payment-processor pressure removed NSFW games (2025). (G, **Med**) [V] https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/valve-tweaks-and-clarifies-ai-disclosure-rules-for-steam
- **Discord bots:** verification to grow past 100 servers (apply from 75); privileged intents must be justified (use slash commands instead of message content); privacy policy, no selling data, deletion on request [K]; Discord users must be 13+, so a parent owns the bot if the builder is younger [K]. (A/G bots, **Med**) [V] https://discord.com/blog/the-future-of-bots-on-discord
- **AI providers for apps used by minors (Anthropic):** under-18 apps need age verification, moderation, monitoring and reporting, COPPA compliance, and must say so publicly; report apparent CSAM to NCMEC. Breaching usage policies can kill the API key (and the app). (A/M, **Med–High**) [V] https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15591275-child-safety-guidance-for-developers

### Copyright, licences, domains, email
- **AI training and memorisation:** *GEMA v. OpenAI* (Munich, Nov 2025): a model reproducing lyrics infringes, the TDM exception doesn't cover it; *Bartz v. Anthropic* settled for $1.5B over pirated books. Never fine-tune or build a RAG index on pirated/scraped copyrighted sets; block full lyrics or book text in output. Game/stream music needs a sync licence ("royalty-free" ≠ free for streaming) [K]; desktop font licences often exclude app/game embedding [K]. (A/G, **Med**) [V] https://ktslaw.com/insights/alert/2025/9/parties-reach-a-landmark-settlement-in-the-bartz-v-anthropic-litigation
- **AI-generated code and licences [K]:** assistants can reproduce GPL snippets verbatim; turn on "block public code"/duplicate detection where available and licence-scan your own repo, not just dependencies. (all, **Low–Med**)
- **Domain privacy:** `.us` domains forbid WHOIS privacy (real name and address public). Use a .com with registrar redaction, registered by a parent (most registrars require 18+ [K]). (all, **Med** privacy risk for a minor) [V] https://about.us/policies/us-privacy-services-policy
- **Email deliverability (enforced like law):** Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft require SPF + DKIM + DMARC, one-click `List-Unsubscribe`, unsubscribes honoured in 2 days, complaints <0.3%; Gmail rejects non-compliant mail since Nov 2025. Use a proper email provider and your own domain. (S/St, **Med**) [V] https://redsift.com/resources/blog/gmails-enforcement-ramps-up-what-bulk-senders-need-to-know
- **App/SDK "cookies" [K]:** EU ePrivacy covers apps/SDKs reading device IDs or storage: analytics/ad SDKs need consent; iOS cross-app tracking needs the ATT prompt. (Mob, **Med**)

Couldn't verify (treat as ❓): CRA edges for MCP servers and extensions; Google Play trader display; Discord policy wording; Florida/Nebraska/SB 976 thresholds; NY/FL giveaway bonds; FinCEN closed-loop guidance; full CRA date; Apple France encryption declaration; NY A10379 signature.

## 15. Third sweep (2026-10-05)
- **CSAM reporting duty (US 18 USC 2258A, REPORT Act 2024):** any provider with image upload, UGC, chat or AI images must, once it knows of CSAM, report to the NCMEC CyberTipline and preserve the report 1 year; also covers enticement and trafficking. Fines for knowingly not reporting up to $850k/$1M; no small-provider threshold. Prevention: register as an ESP with NCMEC before launch; never download or forward the material; preserve and report; hash-match uploads (Cloudflare's free CSAM Scanning Tool) [K]. UK: report to the IWF. AI-generated CSAM is illegal in the US [K]; UK Crime and Policing Act 2026 bans building AI tools for it and "nudify" tools. (UGC, uploads, AI images, **Crit**) [V] https://www.congress.gov/118/plaws/publ59/PLAW-118publ59.htm
- **Creating intimate deepfakes is a crime (UK, since 6 Feb 2026):** creating OR requesting a non-consensual intimate image of a real adult is an offence even if never shared; nudify tools banned. Prevention: no img2img/face-swap of real people in sexual contexts, NSFW classifier on input and output, ToS ban + fast removal. (A image/video, **Crit**) [V] https://gov.uk/government/news/better-protection-for-victims-thanks-to-new-law-on-sexually-explicit-deepfakes
- **UK OSA covers generative AI (Ofcom, Nov 2024):** user-made bots/characters others chat with = user-to-user service; AI that searches websites = search service; both need OSA risk assessments. Ofcom fines small foreign sites (£1.35M, £450k) and separately £50k–£100k for ignoring information requests. Always answer Ofcom letters. (A companions, AI search, **High** with UK users) [V] https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/ofcom-explains-how-the-uk-online-safety-act-will-apply-to-generative-ai/

## 12. Added from Alon's pre-launch list (2026-10-04)
- Kids default design (COPPA + UK Children's Code): a NEUTRAL age question before any account (e.g. "enter your birth year", not "are you over 13? yes/no" and no hint which answer unlocks more). Under 13 → guest only: no email, no public name, nothing stored that identifies them. Everyone: private by default, leaderboards and friends opt-in, no chat with strangers. (G, High)
- Privacy page in plain words: what is stored, why, how long, who it's shared with (list processors: Supabase, Netlify, Stripe...), how to delete, and a section for parents. (all, High)
- Working "delete my account" that removes EVERYTHING (auth user, profile, scores, uploads in storage); required by GDPR right to erasure and by Apple/Google for apps with accounts. Test it, then check the rows are really gone. (all, High)
- No trackers or ads (especially with kids); self-host fonts (see Germany/Google Fonts). (all, Med)
- Legal pages set: Terms, Privacy, and an accessibility statement (what level you aim for, known gaps, how to report a problem). The statement is good practice and lowers ADA lawsuit risk for US stores; the EAA usually does not apply to microenterprises. (all, Med)
