# Bahamas (researched 2026-10-07, by Claude Opus 5.5 with web search)
General notes, not legal advice. See the disclaimer in README.md in this folder.
Severity: Low today (old 2003 law barely reaches foreign apps); Med once the Data Protection Act 2025 starts (GDPR-style, reaches foreign apps). VAT: Med if you sell digital products to Bahamians.
Marks: [V-reg] regulator/official text, [V-sec] law firm/news/tax vendor, [K] memory.

## Privacy
- [V-sec] Law in force today: Data Protection (Privacy of Personal Information) Act 2003 (Ch. 324A), in operation since 2 Apr 2007. Regulator: Data Protection Commissioner (Chambers 2026, Offlist).
- [V-sec] 2003 Act reaches a foreign company only if it is established in the Bahamas or uses equipment in the Bahamas for processing (then it must name a local representative). A foreign app on foreign servers is mostly outside it (Chambers 2026, Offlist).
- [V-sec] Data Protection Act 2025: passed by Parliament Dec 2025; repeals the 2003 Act once the Minister brings it into force by Gazette notice, with phased commencement and a transition period (Chambers 2026, Nassau Guardian, Kiteworks brief). No commencement notice found as of Aug 2026 (Offlist).
- [V-sec] 2025 Act (when in force): GDPR-based; reaches foreign controllers that offer goods/services to, or monitor, people in the Bahamas; access/correction/erasure/objection rights; registration of all controllers and processors; Commissioner oversees international transfers (s.54); 72-hour breach notice (ss.44, 48); fines up to BSD 50,000 on summary conviction for some offences (Chambers 2026, Kiteworks, ewnews/OPM).
- [V-sec] 2025 Act has special rules for children's data (Tribune / OPM debate reports). Exact age not confirmed.
- [K] No cookie-specific law. Treat analytics/ads cookies as personal data under the 2025 Act once it starts.

## Kids and games
- [K] No game-time or loot-box law. Gambling is restricted for Bahamian residents; no cash-out items or real-money prizes.

## Selling
- [V-sec] VAT 10% (cut from 12% in 2022) applies to digital/electronic services sold by non-residents to Bahamian consumers; rules date from about 2019 (VATCalc, PayPro Global).
- [V-sec] Threshold: sources disagree. VATCalc says register when approaching BSD 100,000/year; another vendor says from the first sale. B2B: reverse charge, no VAT from foreign seller (VATCalc).
- [V-sec] Consumer Protection Act (Ch. 337C): store policy can't override the Act; "store credit only" for returned goods is a breach; defective goods can be returned (Consumer Protection Commission via Nassau Guardian). No online cooling-off right found.
- Practical: use a Merchant of Record (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy) so VAT is handled for you.

## AI, content and platforms
- [V-sec] No AI law found. CARICOM endorsed a non-binding Caribbean AI Policy Roadmap (Jun 2026) (CPA UK).

## What a small foreign dev should consider
- Today: low risk. Basic privacy policy, delete-on-request, secure storage.
- Prepare for the 2025 Act: it reaches foreign apps serving Bahamians, has a 72-hour breach rule and registration. Watch for the Gazette start date.
- Ask for parent consent (or block) for young children; the age isn't confirmed yet.
- Merchant of Record for paid sales (10% VAT).

## Upcoming
- [V-sec] Data Protection Act 2025 commencement: date set by Gazette notice, not yet announced (as of Aug 2026).
