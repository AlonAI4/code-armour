# Cabo Verde (researched 2026-10-07, by Claude Opus 5.5 with web search)
General notes, not legal advice. See the disclaimer in README.md in this folder.
Severity: Med on paper (EU-style data law with a working regulator, VAT for listed foreign e-commerce sellers, 2024 digital services law reaching foreign providers), Low in practice for a tiny foreign app.
Marks: [V-reg] regulator/official text, [V-sec] law firm/news/tax vendor, [K] memory.

## Privacy
- [V-sec] Law 133/V/2001 (general data protection regime), amended by Law 41/VIII/2013 and Law 121/IX/2021 (aligns it with Council of Europe Convention 108+). Regulator: Comissão Nacional de Proteção de Dados (CNPD), operating since 2015; its powers are in Law 42/VIII/2013, amended by Law 120/IX/2021 (DLA Piper, DataGuidance, Boletim Oficial index via search).
- [V-sec] Reach: applies to foreign entities, except those only transiting data through the country (Data Protection Africa). Some vendor sites say foreign controllers targeting Cabo Verde must name a local representative; not confirmed.
- [V-sec] Consent or another legal basis (contract, legitimate interest, etc.). Sensitive data (health, religion, biometrics) generally needs consent, and some processing needs prior CNPD authorisation or registration (DLA Piper).
- [V-sec] Breach: DLA Piper says notify the CNPD within 72 hours unless low risk; Data Protection Africa says no breach duty. Sources conflict (the 72-hour rule may come from the 2021 amendment).
- [V-sec] Transfers abroad: only to countries with adequate protection; otherwise consent, contract necessity or a CNPD-allowed exception (DLA Piper, Data Protection Africa). No localisation rule found.
- [V-sec] Penalties: criminal (up to 2 years' prison), administrative fines and civil liability (DLA Piper).
- [V-sec] Direct e-marketing needs opt-in consent (Law 132/V/2001, electronic communications privacy) (DLA Piper).
- [K] Cookies: Law 132/V/2001 is modelled on the old EU ePrivacy rules, so expect consent for non-essential cookies. Not confirmed.
- [V-sec] Children: no specific age for consent found in any source.

## Kids and games
- [K] Age of majority 18. No loot-box or game-time law found. If unsure, get parent consent for under-18s.

## Selling
- [V-sec] Law 41/X/2024 (Boletim Oficial I Série no. 78, 12 Aug 2024): legal regime for digital services and e-commerce, inspired by the EU Digital Services Act. It covers intermediary-service providers established in Cabo Verde and those offering services there wherever they are based (search summary of the Boletim Oficial).
- [V-sec] VAT 15% on distance sales and digital services by non-residents to people in Cabo Verde (Law 4/X/2021, from 1 Jan 2022). Taxpayers are the foreign e-commerce operators the Ministry of Finance puts on a list (built from bank data), so a tiny seller is unlikely to be listed (PwC, VATCalc). Vendor PayPro says all non-residents must register; sources conflict.
- [V-sec] If registered: local representative, quarterly returns, no input VAT recovery (PayPro, VATCalc).
- [K] Consumer law: Law 88/V/98 (consumer defence). No online cooling-off period confirmed; Portugal's 14-day rule does not apply here.
- Practical: Merchant of Record; show your business details and a clear refund policy.

## AI, content and platforms
- [V-sec] Law 41/X/2024 brings DSA-style duties for intermediary platforms (hosting user content, marketplaces). Exact notice-and-takedown deadlines not found.
- [K] No AI law found.

## What a small foreign dev should consider
- Privacy policy, consent where needed, and a 72-hour breach plan (safest reading).
- Be careful with sensitive data (health etc.): it may need CNPD authorisation.
- Parent consent for minors (no age set; 18 is safe).
- Merchant of Record for paid sales.
- If users post content, have a way to report and remove illegal content.
