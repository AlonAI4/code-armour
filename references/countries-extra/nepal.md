# Nepal (researched 2026-10-05, by Claude Opus 5.5 with web search)
General notes, not legal advice. See the disclaimer in README.md in this folder.
Severity: Low for a small foreign-run app. Main real-world risk: the government blocking unregistered platforms (it did in Sep 2025).
Marks: [V-reg] regulator/official text, [V-sec] law firm/news/tax vendor, [K] memory.

## Privacy
- [V-sec] Individual Privacy Act 2075 (2018), in force 18 Sep 2018. Consent needed to collect/use personal data. No implementing rules issued yet (lawalpine, Pioneer Law).
- [K] Weak extraterritorial reach and no active regulator; no breach-notice deadline; no general data protection authority.
- [K] Children: Children's Act 2075 protects under-18s' privacy; get parent consent for kids.

## Kids and games
- [K] No game-time or loot-box law found. Gambling (incl. online) is largely illegal: no cash-out items.

## Selling
- [V-sec] VAT 13% on electronic services by non-residents to Nepali consumers; register within 30 days once over NPR 3M (~USD 21k) in 12 months; fine NPR 20,000 per failure. IRD notice updated 12 Aug 2025 (Bloomberg Tax).
- [V-sec] Separate 2% Digital Service Tax on non-resident B2C digital income above NPR 3M/yr (threshold raised from 2M).
- Practical: a small dev is below NPR 3M; a Merchant of Record covers it anyway.

## AI, content and platforms
- [V-sec] Sep 2025: 26 unregistered platforms (Facebook, YouTube, X...) blocked under a registration directive (local contact, grievance officer); reversed 9 Sep 2025 after deadly Gen Z protests.
- [V-sec] Social Media Bill 2081 withdrawn by the interim government (Feb 2026).
- [K] Electronic Transactions Act 2063 criminalises some online content; a National AI Policy (2025) exists, no AI law.

## What a small foreign dev should consider
- Consent-based privacy notice; parent consent for kids.
- Moderate user content (report button).
- Sales: nothing to do below NPR 3M/yr.
- If you run a social/chat platform that gets big in Nepal, watch for registration directives.

## Upcoming
- [K] New social media / data protection legislation likely after the 2026 elections; nothing dated found.

## Unverified
- Whether the 2023 social media directive still formally applies to small foreign apps.
- Any breach or children's-data rule.
