# Cote d'Ivoire (researched 2026-10-05, by Claude Sonnet 5.5 with web search)
Severity: Med for a small foreign-run app: foreign digital VAT with no threshold; ARTCI wants prior declarations.
Note: no regulator site fetched (artci.ci, dgi.gouv.ci not read). [V-sec] = secondary page seen today; [K] = memory.

## Privacy
- [V-sec] Law 2013-450 of 19 Jun 2013; regulator ARTCI. Consent, purpose limits, rights of access, correction, objection and erasure. (consentstack, DLA Piper)
- [V-sec] ARTCI says all processing needs prior declaration or authorisation, including geolocation, surveillance and transfers of personal data abroad. (CIO Mag)
- [V-sec] ARTCI launched CERTINUM, a national compliance platform for data protection (title seen only). (Droit Media Finance)
- [K] Children's age and breach deadline not confirmed; foreign reach is plausible via "targeting Ivorian users" but not confirmed.

## Kids and games
- [K] No game-time or social age law found; no loot-box rule found.

## Selling
- [V-sec] VAT 18%. Note of 9 Oct 2023: non-resident digital providers (online ads, data, marketplaces, digital content, gaming, cloud, social networks with paid services) must register and collect VAT for B2C and B2B, no threshold, registration before the first sale. (KPMG, vatcalc)
- [K] Consumer law 2016-412. Stripe not available for Ivorian sellers; mobile money (Orange, MTN, Wave) dominates.

## AI, content and platforms
- [V-sec] Cybercrime Law 2013-451 sets online-provider duties; reported as toughened and consolidated in 2025. (Osiris, WeAreTech)
- [K] No AI law.

## What a small foreign dev realistically must do
- French privacy policy, consent, deletion route; parent consent for kids.
- Direct sales to Ivorian buyers: register for 18% VAT or use a Merchant of Record or app stores.
- Moderate chat; report button; cooperate with takedown requests.
