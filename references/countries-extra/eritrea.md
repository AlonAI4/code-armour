# Eritrea (researched 2026-10-07, by Claude Opus 5.5 with web search)
General notes, not legal advice. See the disclaimer in README.md in this folder.
Severity: Low (no data protection law; US Eritrea sanctions ended Sep 2026; very few people online).
Marks: [V-reg] regulator/official text, [V-sec] law firm/news/tax vendor, [K] memory.

## Privacy
- [V-sec] No data protection law in force and no data protection authority (dataprotection.africa factsheet, updated May 2022; IAPP via search summary). No bill found.
- [V-sec] 1997 Constitution, Art. 18: privacy of communications and correspondence (dataprotection.africa). [K] The 1997 Constitution has never been implemented.
- [V-sec] Not a party to Convention 108 or the Malabo Convention (law.ungovr.org).
- [K] No cookie, breach or transfer rules.

## Kids and games
- [K] No children's data or online-age rules. Age of majority 18. No loot-box law.

## Selling
- [K] No VAT; a sales tax applies locally. No tax on foreign digital services found.
- [V-sec] PayPal lists Eritrea as a PayPal country (paypal.com). Not on Stripe's prohibited-jurisdiction list (stripe.com).
- [K] In practice almost no one in Eritrea has an internationally usable card, and internet access is very limited and state-controlled. Expect almost no paying users.

## Sanctions (as of 2026-10-07)
- [V-sec] UN: all UN measures on Eritrea (arms embargo, travel bans, asset freezes) ended by Resolution 2444, 14 Nov 2018 (UN News).
- [V-sec] US: OFAC removed everyone designated under EO 14046 (2021, Ethiopia/Eritrea crisis: Eritrean Defence Forces, the ruling PFDJ, Red Sea Trading Corporation, Hidri Trust, officials) from the SDN list on Fri 18 Sep 2026, and removed the program's guidance/FAQs. Other US restrictions (e.g. human-rights listings) are not covered by that action (Dawan Africa, Capital Ethiopia via search, AP via search).
- [V-sec] EU: Eritrea-specific regime repealed in 2018. Some Eritrea-linked people/entities were listed under the EU Global Human Rights regime in Mar 2021; current status not confirmed.
- Effect for a small app: targeted only, never a broad embargo. Free sign-ups and paid sales to ordinary users are not banned. Don't deal with anyone on a list. See `sanctioned-places.md`.

## AI, content and platforms
- [K] No AI law. [V-sec] Penal Code has only limited computer-crime offences; a Cybercrime Proclamation's status is unclear (Council of Europe Octopus profile, law.ungovr.org).
- [K] The state controls media tightly; don't expect any platform rules to be enforced against foreign apps.

## What a small foreign dev should consider
- No local privacy law: your home-country rules (or GDPR-style basics) are enough.
- Payments will rarely work; don't plan revenue here.
- Run normal sanctions screening for business deals; ordinary users are fine.
